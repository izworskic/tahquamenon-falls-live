    PRACTICAL GUIDE

    The answers people actually need before the drive.

    How long?

    45 min: Upper Falls. 90 min: Upper Falls plus another overlook or food. 3 hr: both falls. Half day: add island or longer walk. Full day: add serious trail time or Rivermouth.

    Accessibility

    Both falls have accessible viewing opportunities. Upper Falls includes a 1,100-foot accessible boardwalk. Lower Falls has accessible viewpoints and the Ronald A. Olson Island Bridge. A track chair may be available; confirm with the park.

    River Trail

    The point-to-point River Trail is about 5.1 miles. Roots, hills and stairs make it a real hike. Verify seasonal shuttle operation before relying on a one-way plan.

    With kids

    Use Upper Falls as the signature stop, but budget more time at Lower Falls for the island, bridge, multiple cascades and room to move.

    Food

    The Tahquamenon Falls Brewery & Pub is in the Upper Falls area, making it easy to pair with the main overlook without adding another drive.

    Winter

    Groomed skiing, snowshoeing and winter waterfall views make this more than a summer stop. Use the XC tool for regional snow intelligence, then verify local grooming.

    FAQ

    Quick answers.

    Can I see both Upper and Lower Falls in one visit?

    Yes. They are about four road miles apart, and a three-hour visit is a practical baseline for seeing both without rushing every stop.

    Which falls should I prioritize?

    Upper Falls is the stronger default if time is limited. Lower Falls is better when you want to explore, move around or give kids more to do.

    Do I need to hike between the falls?

    No. You can drive between the Upper and Lower day-use areas. The River Trail is an optional point-to-point hike.

    Is Tahquamenon worth visiting in winter?

    Yes. Winter adds frozen-waterfall scenery, groomed cross-country skiing, marked snowshoeing and a quieter park experience. Verify current access and grooming before the drive.
